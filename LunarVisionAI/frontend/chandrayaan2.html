Chandrayaan-2: The Audacious Voyage to the Moon

Launched on 22 July 2019, Chandrayaan-2 represented India's most complex interplanetary mission: carrying an Orbiter, the Vikram Lander, and the Pragyan Rover. While the lander encountered a heartbreaking descent anomaly at 2.1 km altitude, the Orbiter succeeded with flying colors—pioneering ultra-resolution 0.25 m/pixel radar and camera mapping that directly paved the path for Chandrayaan-3's historic touchdown.

The 6 Chapters of the Mission

Step-by-step pictorial chronicle of Chandrayaan-2: from fiery liftoff and trans-lunar flight to the descent anomaly and the enduring triumph of the OHRC orbiter.

STAGE 01 • LIFTOFF

The Roar of 'Bahubali' (GSLV Mk III-M1)

22 JULY 2019 • 14:43:12 IST
GSLV Mk III-M1 at Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota SDSC SHAR

Standing on the Second Launch Pad at Sriharikota, India's heaviest rocket—the GSLV Mk III (nicknamed "Bahubali")—roared to life with twin S200 solid rocket boosters, producing a titanic 8,000 kN of thrust. It injected the 3,850 kg Chandrayaan-2 composite spacecraft into an elliptical Earth parking orbit of 169.7 × 45,475 km with pinpoint accuracy.

✦Orbit Raising: 5 planned Earth-bound maneuvers steadily boosted apogee toward 142,975 km.
✦Fuel Conservation: Highly efficient orbital mechanics left the Orbiter with over 7.5 years of surplus propellant.
STAGE 02 • TRANS-LUNAR CRUISE

First Glimpse of Home: LI4 Earth Images

03 AUGUST 2019 • TRANS-LUNAR INJECTION
First photograph of Planet Earth captured by Chandrayaan-2 LI4 camera LI4 SENSOR

As the spacecraft prepared to depart Earth's gravitational sphere of influence, the on-board LI4 camera on the Vikram lander was activated. It beamed back stunning, crystalline portraits of planet Earth showing the Pacific Ocean and American continent, confirming camera calibration and imaging integrity.

Complete orbital trajectory from Earth spirals to Lunar capture ISRO FLIGHT PLAN
✦14 August 2019 (TLI): Trans-Lunar Injection burn of 1,203 seconds hurled the spacecraft into the lunar transfer trajectory.
✦Voyage Distance: Traveled ~384,400 km across deep interplanetary vacuum.
STAGE 03 • LUNAR ARRIVAL

Lunar Orbit Capture & Vikram Separation

20 AUGUST – 02 SEPTEMBER 2019
First ultra-sharp crater terrain beamed by the Chandrayaan-2 OHRC 0.25 M/PX

On 20 August 2019, the main liquid engine fired for 1,738 seconds in a critical retrograde burn to achieve Lunar Orbit Insertion (LOI). Chandrayaan-2 was captured into a 114 × 18,072 km lunar orbit. Subsequent retrograde maneuvers trimmed it into a pristine circular 100 × 100 km polar orbit passing over both lunar poles every 2 hours.

✦02 September 2019: The Vikram lander smoothly separated from the Orbiter into a 35 × 101 km descent orbit.
✦Historic Camera Benchmark: The Orbiter High Resolution Camera (OHRC) achieved 0.25 m/px resolution—surpassing NASA's LROC NAC (0.5 m/px).
STAGE 04 • POWERED DESCENT

The "15 Minutes of Terror": What Happened

07 SEPTEMBER 2019 • 01:38 IST TO 01:53 IST

Powered descent commenced from 30 km altitude. The autonomous landing sequence was broken down into 4 synchronized phases:

✓Phase 1 (Rough Braking): Dropped from 30 km to 7.4 km. Speed dropped from 1,680 m/s to 146 m/s. Performed 100% perfectly.
⚠Phase 2 (Attitude Hold Phase): Velocity error began building up slightly due to uneven 800 N throttle variations.
✗Phase 3 (Fine Braking Anomaly): At 2.1 km above the Moon, the onboard guidance software encountered a critical trajectory divergence conflict.
✗Loss of Signal (01:52 IST): Telemetry flatlined at 2.1 km altitude. Vikram impacted at ~50 m/s.
STAGE 05 • ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Technical Forensic: Why Did Vikram Crash?

EXPERT COMMITTEE FINDINGS (DR. SIVAN & ISRO SAC)
NASA LRO / ISRO forensic verification showing Vikram impact point (~500m off-target) CRASH SITE

ISRO constituted a high-level National Failure Analysis Committee. The investigation revealed three compounding technical flaws rather than a single engine hardware failure:

1. Thruster Calibration Drift: The five 800 N variable-thrust liquid engines produced slightly higher thrust than modeled, causing altitude to shed faster than planned.
2. Trajectory Correction Clamp: The control software had a tight rate-limit on orientation changes (<10°/sec). As the lander drifted off the nominal corridor, it couldn't turn aggressively enough to brake horizontally.
3. Landing Site Search Logic: To reach the designated 500 m × 500 m safe zone, the computer prioritized high horizontal translation rather than reducing vertical descent velocity, causing it to strike the surface at ~50 m/s (~180 km/h).
STAGE 06 • THE REDEMPTION

The 98% Success: How Ch-2 Won Chandrayaan-3

2019 – 2023 • SHIV SHAKTI POINT

While public attention was fixed on the lander, the Chandrayaan-2 Orbiter achieved 98% of the mission's scientific objectives and remains fully operational today!

✓ Pioneering Water-Ice Discovery: Dual-frequency SAR and CLASS/OHRC sensors mapped volatile ice signatures inside Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs).
✓ The Eyes for Chandrayaan-3: Chandrayaan-2's OHRC camera spent 4 years mapping the lunar south pole at 0.25 m/px, pinpointing hazard-free zones and expanding the landing box from 500 m to 4 km × 2.4 km.
✓ Failure-Based Redesign: ISRO Chairman S. Somanath converted "success-based" architecture into "failure-based" redundancies in Chandrayaan-3 (wider legs, 4 thrusters, velocity tolerance up to 3 m/s).
✓ Historic Touchdown (23 August 2023): Chandrayaan-3 landed flawlessly at Shiv Shakti Point (69.37°S, 32.35°E)—communicating through the orbiting Chandrayaan-2 relay!
